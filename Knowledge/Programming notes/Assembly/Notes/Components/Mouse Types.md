# 🖱️ Mouse Types

> [!abstract] Overview A computer mouse controls the cursor on the screen. Different mice use different tracking technologies, connection methods, and designs for comfort, office work, or gaming.

---

## ⚙️ Mechanical Mouse

A **mechanical mouse** uses a physical ball on its underside to detect movement. As the mouse moves, the ball rotates and internal rollers track its direction.

### Key Features

- **Physical tracking ball:** Detects movement through mechanical parts.
- **Older technology:** Common before optical mice became widespread.
- **Maintenance:** The ball and rollers can collect dust and require cleaning.
- **Today:** Mostly replaced by optical and laser mice.

### Examples

- Early Microsoft ball mice
- Older Logitech ball mice

**Buying Recommendation:** Mainly found as used or vintage models. Not recommended for most modern setups.

## 🔴 Optical Mouse

![[Pasted image 20260710111111.png|375]]

An **optical mouse** uses a light source, usually an LED, and an image sensor to detect movement across a surface.

### Key Features

- **Light-based tracking:** Detects small changes in the surface beneath it.
- **Common technology:** Used in most everyday computer mice.
- **Low maintenance:** Has no moving tracking ball.
- **Surface compatibility:** Works well on many ordinary surfaces, but may struggle with glass or highly reflective materials.

### Examples

- Standard office mouse
- Basic desktop mouse

### Mouse Examples

- Logitech M100
- Logitech B100
- HP X500

> [!tip] Remember **Optical = Uses light and a sensor to track movement.**

---

## 🔦 Laser Mouse

![[Pasted image 20260710111337.png|272]]

A **laser mouse** uses a laser-based optical sensor to track movement. It works similarly to a standard optical mouse but uses a different light source.

### Key Features

- **Laser tracking:** Uses laser illumination to detect surface details.
- **Surface compatibility:** Some models work on surfaces that ordinary optical mice struggle with.
- **Precision:** Performance depends on the sensor and mouse design.
- **Modern use:** Found in some premium office and productivity mice.

### Mouse Examples

- Logitech MX Master 3S
- Logitech MX Anywhere 3S

> [!tip] Remember **Laser = A type of optical tracking that uses a laser light source.**

> [!note] Important Laser mice are not automatically more accurate than LED optical mice. Actual performance depends on the sensor, surface, and implementation.

---

## 🔌 Wired Mouse

![[Pasted image 20260710111430.png|257]]

A **wired mouse** connects directly to the computer using a cable, usually USB.

### Key Features

- **Direct connection:** Usually connects through USB.
- **No mouse battery:** Receives power through the cable.
- **Reliable connection:** No wireless interference.
- **Low latency:** Commonly preferred for responsive gaming.

### Examples

- Standard desktop mouse
- Wired gaming mouse

### Mouse Examples

- Logitech G203
- Razer DeathAdder Essential
- Logitech M100

> [!tip] Remember **Wired = Connects through a cable and usually needs no battery.**

---

## 📡 Wireless Mouse

![[Pasted image 20260710111456.png|336]]

A **wireless mouse** connects to a computer without a physical data cable.

### Connection Types

- **Bluetooth:** Connects directly to a computer with Bluetooth support.
- **USB receiver:** Uses a small receiver plugged into a USB port.

### Key Features

- **Portable:** Easy to carry with a laptop.
- **Cable-free:** Keeps the desk tidy.
- **Power source:** Usually uses batteries or a rechargeable battery.
- **Convenience:** Easy to move around without a cable.

### Mouse Examples

- Logitech M185
- Logitech Pebble Mouse 2 M350s
- Microsoft Bluetooth Mouse

> [!tip] Remember **Wireless = Connects through Bluetooth or a wireless receiver.**

---

## 🎮 Gaming Mouse

![[Pasted image 20260710111529.png|391]]

A **gaming mouse** is designed for responsive control, precise tracking, and additional features useful in games.

### Key Features

- **High-performance sensor:** Tracks movement accurately.
- **Adjustable DPI:** Changes how far the cursor moves relative to physical mouse movement.
- **Extra buttons:** Can be programmed for shortcuts or in-game actions.
- **Polling rate:** Determines how frequently the mouse reports its position to the computer.
- **Ergonomic designs:** Often shaped for particular grip styles.

### Mouse Examples

- Logitech G Pro X Superlight 2
- Razer DeathAdder V3
- SteelSeries Rival 3

> [!tip] Remember **Gaming = Designed for responsive tracking, customization, and control.**

> [!note] DPI Explained DPI stands for _dots per inch_. In mouse settings, it describes tracking sensitivity: higher DPI generally moves the cursor farther with the same physical movement.

---

## ✋ Vertical Mouse

![[Pasted image 20260710111621.png|268]]

A **vertical mouse** has an angled shape that positions the hand more like a handshake than a traditional flat mouse.

### Key Features

- **Ergonomic shape:** Encourages a different hand position.
- **Reduced forearm rotation:** Can reduce the need to rotate the forearm palm-down.
- **Comfort:** May help some users during long computer sessions.
- **Adjustment period:** Can take time to get used to.

### Mouse Examples

- Logitech Lift
- Logitech MX Vertical

> [!tip] Remember **Vertical = Designed to encourage a more natural hand position.**

---

## 🔵 Trackball Mouse

![[Pasted image 20260710111658.png|344]]

A **trackball mouse** stays in one place while you move the cursor by rotating a ball with your fingers or thumb.

### Key Features

- **Stationary design:** The device itself does not need to move.
- **Ball control:** Finger or thumb movement controls the cursor.
- **Small desk space:** Requires less room than a traditional mouse.
- **Ergonomic options:** Some designs reduce the amount of arm movement required.

### Mouse Examples

- Logitech ERGO M575
- Kensington Expert Mouse Wireless Trackball

> [!tip] Remember **Trackball = Move the ball, not the mouse.**

---

## 🧠 Quick Comparison

|Mouse Type|Main Feature|Best Suited For|
|---|---|---|
|Mechanical|Physical tracking ball|Vintage systems|
|Optical|LED-based optical tracking|Everyday use|
|Laser|Laser-based optical tracking|Some productivity setups|
|Wired|Cable connection|Reliable, low-latency use|
|Wireless|Bluetooth or USB receiver|Portability and convenience|
|Gaming|Responsive sensor and extra features|Gaming and customization|
|Vertical|Angled ergonomic shape|Alternative hand positioning|
|Trackball|Stationary ball control|Small desks and reduced arm movement|
