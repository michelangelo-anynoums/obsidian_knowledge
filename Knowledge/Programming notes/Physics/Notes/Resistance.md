# 3.4 Resistance

## **Resistance**

- **Resistance** is the **opposition to the flow of electric current**.
    
- It makes it **harder** for electrons to move through a material.
    
- The **unit of resistance** is the **ohm (Ω)**.
    

**Formula:**

```text
R = V / I
```

Where:

- **R** = resistance (**Ω**)
    
- **V** = voltage (**V**)
    
- **I** = current (**A**)
    

**Example:**

- A **100 Ω resistor** slows the flow of current more than a **10 Ω resistor**.
    

---

## **Opposition to Current Flow**

- A **high resistance** means **less current** can flow.
    
- A **low resistance** means **more current** can flow.
    
- Resistance **controls the amount of current** in a circuit.
    

**Example:**

- A thin wire usually has **more resistance** than a thick wire.
    

---

## **Material Properties Affecting Resistance**

Resistance depends on:

- **Material** – **Copper** has **low resistance**, while **rubber** has **high resistance**.
    
- **Length** – A **longer wire** has **more resistance**.
    
- **Thickness** – A **thicker wire** has **less resistance**.
    
- **Temperature** – In most metals, **higher temperature** means **higher resistance**.
    

**Example:**

- A long, thin wire resists the flow of current more than a short, thick wire.
    

---

## **Heat Generation in Resistors**

- When current flows through a **resistor**, some **electrical energy** changes into **heat**.
    
- This is called **Joule heating**.
    
- The more current flows, the **more heat** is produced.
    

**Formula:**

```text
P = I² × R
```

Where:

- **P** = power (**W**)
    
- **I** = current (**A**)
    
- **R** = resistance (**Ω**)
    

**Example:**

- The heating element in an **electric heater** becomes hot because of its **resistance**.
    

> **Remember:**
> 
> - **High resistance → Less current**
>     
> - **Low resistance → More current**
>     
> - **Resistance converts electrical energy into heat.**

# 3.4 Resistance — Resolved Exercises

## Resistance

### Exercise 1

A resistor has:

- V = 12 V
    
- I = 3 A
    

Calculate the resistance.

**Solution:**

Formula:

**R = V / I**

**R = 12 / 3**

**R = 4 Ω**

**Answer:** The resistance is **4 Ω**.

---

### Exercise 2

A circuit has a resistance of **10 Ω** and a voltage of **20 V**. Calculate the current.

**Solution:**

Formula:

**I = V / R**

**I = 20 / 10**

**I = 2 A**

**Answer:** The current is **2 A**.

# Opposition to Current Flow

### Exercise 1

Which resistor allows more current to flow: **5 Ω** or **50 Ω**?

**Solution:**

Lower resistance allows more current.

**Answer:** The **5 Ω resistor** allows more current.

---

### Exercise 2

Why does a thin wire have more resistance than a thick wire?

**Solution:**

A thinner wire gives electrons less space to move.

**Answer:** A thin wire has **higher resistance**.

### Exercise 1

Which has lower resistance: copper or rubber?

**Solution:**

Copper is a good conductor, while rubber is an insulator.

**Answer:** **Copper** has lower resistance.

A wire becomes longer. What happens to its resistance?

**Solution:**

Longer wires make it harder for electrons to move.

# Heat Generation in Resistors

### Exercise 1

A resistor has:

- I = 2 A
    
- R = 5 Ω
    

Calculate the heat power produced.

**Solution:**

Formula:

**P = I² × R**

**P = 2² × 5**

**P = 4 × 5**

**P = 20 W**

**Answer:** The resistor produces **20 W** of heat power.

---

### Exercise 2

Why does an electric heater become hot?

**Solution:**

Current passing through a resistor changes electrical energy into heat.

**Answer:** The resistance produces **Joule heating**.
