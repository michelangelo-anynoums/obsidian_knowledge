# 1.2 Scale & Orders of Magnitude

## Nanoseconds (ns)

A **nanosecond (ns)** is **one billionth of a second** (0.000000001 s). Computers use nanoseconds to measure **CPU timing** because processors work extremely fast.

## Gigahertz (GHz)

**Gigahertz (GHz)** measures **processor frequency**. It shows how many **billions of cycles per second** a CPU can perform. A **higher GHz** usually means a **faster processor**.

## Nanometers (nm)

A **nanometer (nm)** is **one billionth of a meter** (0.000000001 m). It is used to measure the **size of transistors** inside computer chips. **Smaller transistors** can improve **speed** and **energy efficiency**.

## Decibels (dB)

**Decibels (dB)** measure **signal strength** or **sound level**. In computers and networks, they show how **strong or weak a signal** is. A **higher dB** usually means a **stronger signal**.

Common examples include **whispers** at 20–40 dB, **normal conversation** at 60 dB, **rock concerts** at 120 dB, and **jet engines** at 140 dB.


---

For **Wi-Fi** and **radio signals**, decibels are typically measured in **dBm** (decibels relative to 1 milliwatt).

Wi-Fi signal strength (RSSI) generally ranges from **-30 dBm** (maximum strength) to **-90 dBm** (noise floor). 

- **-30 to -50 dBm (Excellent)**: Achieved only when standing next to the router. Supports maximum throughput, 4K/8K streaming, and lag-free gaming. 
    
- **-50 to -67 dBm (Very Good)**: Typical for devices 1–2 rooms away. Reliable for HD video, VoIP calls, and large downloads. **-67 dBm** is often considered the minimum for high-performance tasks. 
    
- **-67 to -70 dBm (Good)**: Suitable for standard web browsing and email. May experience occasional buffering with HD video. 
    
- **-70 to -80 dBm (Fair/Weak)**: Found at the edge of a router's range or through thick walls. Basic connectivity is possible, but latency spikes and packet loss are common. 
    
- **Below -80 dBm (Unusable)**: Considered a "dead zone." Connections frequently drop, and modern devices may refuse to associate.
