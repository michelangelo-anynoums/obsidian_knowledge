# 6.3 Wavelength

## **What is Wavelength?**

The **wavelength** (**λ**, lambda) is the **distance between two identical points** on a wave.

It is usually measured:

- From **crest to crest**.
- From **trough to trough**.

**Unit:** **meter (m)**

**Example:**  
If the distance from one crest to the next is **3 m**, then:

λ=3 m

---

# **Relationship Between Wavelength and Frequency**

The **wavelength** and **frequency** are related by the **wave speed**.

The formula is:

$$v=fλ$$

where:

- **v** = wave speed (**m/s**)
- **f** = frequency (**Hz**)
- **λ** = wavelength (**m**)

- **Higher frequency** → **shorter wavelength** (if wave speed stays constant).
- **Lower frequency** → **longer wavelength**.

**Example:**

- **High-pitched sound** → high frequency, short wavelength.
- **Low-pitched sound** → low frequency, long wavelength.

---

# **Important Ideas**

- **Wavelength** is the **length of one complete wave**.
- It is measured **crest to crest** or **trough to trough**.
- **Unit:** **meter (m)**.
- **Frequency** and **wavelength** are **inversely related** when the wave speed is constant.
- The wave speed is:

$$v=fλ$$

---

# **Resolved Examples**

## **Example 1**

The distance between two wave crests is **5 m**.

**Find the wavelength.**

**Solution**

The wavelength is the distance between two crests.

λ=5 m

**Answer:** **5 m**

---

## **Example 2**

A wave has:

- **f = 10 Hz**
- **λ = 2 m**

**Find the wave speed.**

**Solution**

Formula:

v=fλ

Substitute:

v=10×2=20 m/s

**Answer:** **20 m/s**

---

## **Example 3**

A wave travels at **12 m/s** and has a frequency of **4 Hz**.

**Find the wavelength.**

**Solution**

Formula:

λ=vf

Substitute:

λ=124=3 m

**Answer:** **3 m**
