# 4.4 Inductors

## **What is an inductor?**

An **inductor** is an electrical component that **stores energy in a magnetic field**.

- It is usually made from a **coil of wire**.
    
- When current flows through the coil, it creates a **magnetic field**.
    

**Example:** A coil inside a power supply.

---

## **Store energy in a magnetic field**

An inductor stores **energy** while current flows through it.

**Formula:**

```text
E = ½ × L × I²
```

Where:

- **E** = stored energy (J, joules)
    
- **L** = inductance (H, henries)
    
- **I** = current (A, amperes)
    

**Example:**

```text
L = 2 H
I = 3 A

E = ½ × 2 × 3²
E = 1 × 9
E = 9 J
```

---

## **Oppose changes in current**

An inductor **opposes sudden changes** in current.

- If the current **increases quickly**, the inductor slows the increase.
    
- If the current **decreases quickly**, the inductor tries to keep the current flowing.
    

**Simple idea:**

- **Current increases** → Inductor resists the increase.
    
- **Current decreases** → Inductor resists the decrease.
    

**Formula:**

```text
V = L × (ΔI / Δt)
```

Where:

- **V** = induced voltage (V, volts)
    
- **L** = inductance (H)
    
- **ΔI** = change in current (A)
    
- **Δt** = time (s)
    

---

## **Used in power regulation**

Inductors help **control and smooth electrical current**.

They are commonly used in:

- **Power supplies**
    
- **Voltage regulators**
    
- **DC-DC converters**
    
- **Filters** to reduce electrical noise
    

**Example:** A laptop charger uses inductors to provide a smooth and stable output current.

---

# **Important Facts**

- An **inductor stores energy** in a **magnetic field**.
    
- It **opposes changes in current**.
    
- The unit of inductance is the **henry (H)**.
    
- Inductors are widely used in **power regulation** and **electrical filters**.

# **Resolved Examples**

## **Example 1: Stored Energy**

**Question:**  
An inductor has an inductance of **4 H** and carries a current of **2 A**. How much energy is stored?

**Solution:**

Use the formula:

```text
E = ½ × L × I²
```

Substitute the values:

```text
E = ½ × 4 × 2²
E = 2 × 4
E = 8 J
```

**Answer:**

```text
E = 8 J
```

---

## **Example 2: Find the Inductance**

**Question:**  
An inductor stores **18 J** of energy when the current is **3 A**. Find the inductance.

**Solution:**

Use:

```text
E = ½ × L × I²
```

Rearrange the formula:

```text
L = (2 × E) / I²
```

Substitute the values:

```text
L = (2 × 18) / 3²
L = 36 / 9
L = 4 H
```

**Answer:**

```text
L = 4 H
```

---

## **Example 3: Induced Voltage**

**Question:**  
An inductor has an inductance of **2 H**. The current changes by **6 A** in **3 s**. Find the induced voltage.

**Solution:**

Use the formula:

```text
V = L × (ΔI / Δt)
```

Substitute the values:

```text
V = 2 × (6 / 3)
V = 2 × 2
V = 4 V
```

**Answer:**

```text
V = 4 V
```
