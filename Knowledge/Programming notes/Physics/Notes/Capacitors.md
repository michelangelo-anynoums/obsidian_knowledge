# 4.3 Capacitors

## **What is a capacitor?**

A **capacitor** is an electrical component that **stores electric charge** and **electrical energy**.

- It has **two metal plates** separated by an **insulating material (dielectric)**.
    
- It can **charge** (store energy) and **discharge** (release energy).
    

**Example:** A camera flash stores energy in a capacitor before producing a bright flash.

---

## **Store electric charge**

A capacitor stores **electric charge** when it is connected to a voltage source.

**Formula:**

```text
Q = C × V
```

Where:

- **Q** = electric charge (C, coulombs)
    
- **C** = capacitance (F, farads)
    
- **V** = voltage (V, volts)
    

**Example:**

```text
C = 2 F
V = 6 V

Q = C × V
Q = 2 × 6
Q = 12 C
```

---

## **Charge and discharge behavior**

A capacitor does **not charge or discharge instantly**.

- **Charging:** The capacitor **stores more charge** until it is full.
    
- **Discharging:** The capacitor **releases the stored charge** to the circuit.
    

**Simple idea:**

- **Charging:** Battery → Capacitor
    
- **Discharging:** Capacitor → Circuit
    

**Example:**

A capacitor connected to a battery slowly charges. When the battery is removed and a bulb is connected, the capacitor discharges and the bulb lights for a short time.

---

## **Used in memory and filtering**

Capacitors are used in many electronic devices.

### **Memory**

Some electronic memories use capacitors to **store information** for a short time.

**Example:** Dynamic RAM (**DRAM**) stores each bit of data as charge in a capacitor.

### **Filtering**

Capacitors help **remove unwanted electrical noise** and make the voltage **smoother**.

**Example:** A phone charger uses capacitors to provide a steady output voltage.

---

# **Important Facts**

- A **capacitor stores electric charge**.
    
- A capacitor **charges** and **discharges** over time.
    
- Capacitors are used in **memory circuits** and **electrical filters**.
    
- The unit of capacitance is the **farad (F)**.

# **Resolved Examples**

## **Example 1: Charge Stored**

**Question:**  
A capacitor has a capacitance of **3 F** and is connected to a **4 V** battery. How much charge does it store?

**Solution:**

Use the formula:

```text
Q = C × V
```

Substitute the values:

```text
Q = 3 × 4
Q = 12 C
```

**Answer:**

```text
Q = 12 C
```

---

## **Example 2: Find the Capacitance**

**Question:**  
A capacitor stores **20 C** of charge at **5 V**. Find its capacitance.

**Solution:**

Use:

```text
Q = C × V
```

Rearrange the formula:

```text
C = Q / V
```

Substitute the values:

```text
C = 20 / 5
C = 4 F
```

**Answer:**

```text
C = 4 F
```

---

## **Example 3: Find the Voltage**

**Question:**  
A capacitor has a capacitance of **5 F** and stores **25 C** of charge. Find the voltage.

**Solution:**

Rearrange the formula:

```text
V = Q / C
```

Substitute the values:

```text
V = 25 / 5
V = 5 V
```

**Answer:**

```text
V = 5 V
```
