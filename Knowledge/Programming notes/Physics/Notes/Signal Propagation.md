# 11.5 Signal Propagation

## 📡 What is signal propagation?

**Signal propagation** describes how **radio waves travel through an environment** from a transmitter to a receiver.

When a wave travels, objects and materials can change its path or reduce its strength.

The main effects are:

- **Reflection**
- **Absorption**
- **Scattering**

## 🪞 Reflection

**Reflection** happens when a radio wave **bounces off a surface**.

**Example:** A radio wave can reflect from a **building wall**.

## 🧱 Absorption

**Absorption** happens when a material **takes in some of the wave's energy**.

This makes the signal **weaker**.

**Example:** Walls, furniture, and the human body can absorb some radio-wave energy.

## 🌫️ Scattering

**Scattering** happens when a radio wave is **spread in different directions** by small objects or irregular surfaces.

**Example:** Small objects, particles, or rough surfaces can scatter a radio signal.


## 🔑 Important idea

These effects can change the **strength and quality** of a wireless signal.


## 🧮 Resolved example 1 — Reflection

A Wi-Fi signal reaches a large metal surface.

**Question:** What can happen?

**Solution:**

The signal can **bounce off the surface**.
