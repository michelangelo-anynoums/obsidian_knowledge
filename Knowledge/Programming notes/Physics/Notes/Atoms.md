# ⚛️ Atoms, Electricity & Electromagnetism

> [!abstract] The big idea **Atoms** make up matter. **Electricity** involves electric charges, and **electromagnetism** connects electricity with magnetic fields.

## 1. ⚛️ The Atom

An **atom** is the basic unit of a chemical element. It contains a central nucleus surrounded by electrons.

- **Protons (+):** Positively charged particles in the nucleus.
- **Neutrons (0):** Neutral particles in the nucleus.
- **Electrons (−):** Negatively charged particles around the nucleus.

|Particle|Electric charge|Location|
|---|---|---|
|Proton|Positive (+)|Nucleus|
|Neutron|Neutral (0)|Nucleus|
|Electron|Negative (−)|Around nucleus|

![[Pasted image 20261018145622.png|381]]

**Example:** A neutral carbon atom has 6 protons and 6 electrons. Their opposite charges cancel, giving the atom a total electric charge of zero.

> [!tip] Remember Protons determine which element an atom is. Electrons are especially important for electricity and chemical bonding.

## 2. ⚡ Ions and Electron Transfer

An **ion** is an atom or group of atoms with a net electric charge because it has gained or lost electrons.

- **Cation (+):** Forms when an atom loses electrons.
- **Anion (−):** Forms when an atom gains electrons.

**Example: Sodium and chlorine**

**Sodium (Na)** has one electron in its outermost shell that it can readily lose. **Chlorine (Cl)** needs one electron to complete its outermost shell.

![[Pasted image 20261018150121.png|332]]

$$Na→{Na^+ + e^-}$$

$$Cl+e^−→{Cl^-}$$

Sodium **transfers an electron** to chlorine. The resulting Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions attract each other, forming **ionic sodium chloride** (NaCl), or table salt.

> [!important] Key distinction Losing or gaining electrons creates ions. The number of protons does not change, so the element remains the same.

## 3. 🔋 Electron Transfer and Equilibration

When two different materials come into contact, electrons may transfer from one to the other. This can happen because their electrons have different energies or because the materials have different tendencies to hold electrons.

**What does equilibration mean?**

Electrons tend to redistribute in response to differences in electric potential and energy. When a system reaches equilibrium, there is no net driving force for further charge flow under those conditions.

> [!note] Important Two metals simply touching do not necessarily produce a continuous electric current. A sustained current requires a suitable circuit and, in a battery, chemical reactions that keep driving the process.

## 4. ⚡ Electricity

**Electricity** describes phenomena associated with electric charge, including its presence, forces, and movement.

There are two useful basic concepts:

- **Static electricity:** Charge builds up on a surface.
- **Electric current:** Electric charge flows through a material.

### Electric current

Electric current is the rate at which electric charge passes a point.

$$I = \frac{Q}{t}$$

Where:

- I = current, measured in amperes (A).
- Q = charge, measured in coulombs (C).
- t = time, measured in seconds (s).

### Voltage and resistance

- **Voltage (V):** Electric potential difference; the energy transferred per unit charge.
- **Resistance (R):** How strongly a component opposes current.
- **Current (I):** The rate of charge flow.

For a simple ohmic component, Ohm's law states:

$$V = IR$$

_Think of voltage as the electrical push and resistance as the opposition to charge flow._

## 5. 🧲 Electromagnetism

**Electromagnetism** is the relationship between electric charges, electric currents, and magnetic fields.

**Reference: ** [[Wireless Fundamentals]]

The essential ideas are:

- Electric charges create electric fields.
- Moving charges produce magnetic fields.
- Changing magnetic fields can induce voltages and currents.
- Magnetic forces act on moving charges.

![[Pasted image 20261018150828.png|494]]

> [!important] The connection Electric current can create magnetism, and changing magnetism can generate electricity. This is the foundation of motors, generators, transformers, and many electrical devices.
