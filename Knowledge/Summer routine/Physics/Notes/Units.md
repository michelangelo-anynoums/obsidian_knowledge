# 1.1 Units

## SI units

**SI units** are the standard units used all over the world. They help everyone measure things in the same way.

- **Meter (m)** – measures **length**.
    
- **Second (s)** – measures **time**.
    
- **Ampere (A)** – measures **electric current**.
    
- **Volt (V)** – measures **electrical voltage**.
    

---

The **International System of Units (SI)** is the modern metric system and the world's most widely used standard for measurement.  It is composed of **7 base units**, **2 supplementary units**, and **19 derived units**, totaling 28 defined units.

The **7 SI base units** are:

- **Meter (m)** for length
    
- **Kilogram (kg)** for mass
    
- **Second (s)** for time
    
- **Ampere (A)** for electric current
    
- **Kelvin (K)** for thermodynamic temperature
    
- **Mole (mol)** for amount of substance
    
- **Candela (cd)** for luminous intensity 
    

Common **derived units** include the **Hertz (Hz)** for frequency, **Newton (N)** for force, **Pascal (Pa)** for pressure, **Joule (J)** for energy, **Watt (W)** for power, **Volt (V)** for electric potential, **Ohm (Ω)** for resistance, and **Farad (F)** for capacitance.  Non-SI units such as the **litre (L)** and **tonne (t)** are also accepted for use with the SI.

---

## Derived units

**Derived units** are units of measurement in the **International System of Units (SI)** that are obtained by mathematically combining the seven **SI base units** (meter, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole, and candela) through multiplication, division, or exponentiation.

- **Hertz (Hz)** – measures **frequency** (how often something happens each second).
    
- **Ohm (Ω)** – measures **electrical resistance**.
    
- **Joule (J)** – measures **energy**.
    
- **Watt (W)** – measures **power** (how fast energy is used).
    

## Why computers rely on standardized units

Computers use **standardized units** so that **measurements are accurate**, **devices work together**, and **data is understood the same way** everywhere. This helps make computer systems **reliable** and **consistent**.
