# 8.5 PN Junction

## **PN Junction**

A **PN junction** is formed when a **P-type semiconductor** and an **N-type semiconductor** are joined together.

![[Pasted image 20260730115917.png|595]]

It is the basic structure used to create **diodes** and **transistors**.

At the junction:

- Electrons from the N-side move toward the P-side.
    
- Holes from the P-side move toward the N-side.
    
- A region with no free carriers is created, called the **depletion layer**.
    

The depletion layer acts like a barrier that controls electron movement.

---

## **Basis of diodes**

A **diode** is an electronic component made from a **PN junction**.

Its main function is to allow current to flow in **one direction**.

### **Forward bias**

When the P-side is connected to the positive terminal and the N-side to the negative terminal:

- The depletion layer becomes smaller.
    
- Electrons can cross the junction.
    
- Current flows.
    

$$ 
\text{Current flows} \rightarrow \boxed{\text{ON}}  
$$

Example:

A diode can be used to convert **AC current into DC current**.

---

### **Reverse bias**

When the connections are reversed:

- The depletion layer becomes larger.
    
- Electrons cannot easily cross the junction.
    
- Very little current flows.
    

$$ 
\text{Current blocked} \rightarrow \boxed{\text{OFF}}  
$$

---

## **Basis of transistors**

A **transistor** is made using two PN junctions.

Example:

$$
\text{NPN transistor: } N - P - N  
$$

Transistors can:

- **amplify signals**
    
- **switch electronic circuits**
    

They are the basic components of **computers and digital devices**.

Example:

A computer processor contains billions of tiny transistors working as switches.

---

## **Controlled electron flow**

A PN junction allows us to **control the movement of electrons**.

By changing the voltage:

- The junction can allow current to pass.
    
- The junction can block current.
    

The relationship between voltage and current is:

$$ 
I = \frac{V}{R}  
$$

Where:

- (I) = current (A)
    
- (V) = voltage (V)
    
- (R) = resistance (Ω)
    

---

# Simple comparison

|Component|Structure|Function|
|---|---|---|
|**Diode**|One PN junction|Allows current in one direction|
|**Transistor**|Two PN junctions|Switches or amplifies signals|

# Resolved examples

## Example 1: Diode direction

**Question:**  
A diode is forward biased. Does current flow?

**Solution:**

1. Forward bias reduces the depletion layer.
    
2. Electrons can cross the junction.
    
3. Current can move through the diode.
    

**Answer:**

$$ 
\boxed{\text{Yes, current flows}}  
$$

---

## Example 2: Identifying a component

**Question:**  
A device is made from two PN junctions and works as an electronic switch. What is it?

**Solution:**

1. Two PN junctions are used.
    
2. It controls electron flow.
    
3. It can switch signals.
    

**Answer:**

$$  
\boxed{\text{A transistor}}  
$$

---

## Example 3: Reverse bias

**Question:**  
Why does a reverse-biased diode block current?

**Solution:**

1. The voltage increases the depletion layer.
    
2. Electrons cannot easily cross the junction.
    
3. Current becomes almost zero.
    

**Answer:**

$$ 
\boxed{\text{The depletion layer prevents electron movement}}  
$$
