# 3.1 Electric Charge

## **Electric Charge**

- **Electric charge** is a property of matter that causes **electric forces**.
    
- There are **two types of charge**:
    
    - **Positive (+)**
        
    - **Negative (−)**
        
- The **unit of charge** is the **coulomb (C)**.
    

**Formula:**

```text
Q = n × e
```

Where:

- **Q** = electric charge (**C**)
    
- **n** = number of electrons
    
- **e** = charge of one electron
    

```text
e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
```

**Example:**

- A balloon rubbed on hair becomes **negatively charged** because it gains **electrons**.
    

---

## **Electrons as Charge Carriers**

- **Electrons** are the main **charge carriers** in most materials.
    
- They can **move** through conductors such as **metals**.
    
- The movement of electrons produces an **electric current**.
    

**Formula:**

```text
I = Q / t
```

Where:

- **I** = electric current (**A**)
    
- **Q** = electric charge (**C**)
    
- **t** = time (**s**)
    

**Example:**

- In a wire, **electrons move** from one end to the other, creating an **electric current**.
    

---

## **Positive vs Negative Charge**

- **Positive charge (+)** means an object has **lost electrons**.
    
- **Negative charge (−)** means an object has **gained electrons**.
    
- **Opposite charges attract**.
    
- **Like charges repel**.
    

**Example:**

- A **positive** and a **negative** charge move toward each other.
    
- Two **negative** charges push away from each other.
    

---
## **Ions**

- An **ion** is an **atom** that has **gained** or **lost electrons**.
    
- Ions have an **electric charge**.
    

### **Types of Ions**

- **Cation (+):** an atom **loses electrons** and becomes **positively charged**.
    
- **Anion (−):** an atom **gains electrons** and becomes **negatively charged**.
    

**Examples:**

- **Na → Na⁺ + e⁻** (Sodium loses one electron and becomes a **cation**.)
    
- **Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻** (Chlorine gains one electron and becomes an **anion**.)
    

**Remember:**

- **Loses electrons → Positive ion (cation)**
    
- **Gains electrons → Negative ion (anion)**

---

## **Charge Conservation**

- **Electric charge is conserved**.
    
- **Charge cannot be created or destroyed.**
    
- Charge can only be **transferred** from one object to another.
    

**Formula:**

```text
Q_before = Q_after
```

or

```text
Total charge before = Total charge after
```

**Example:**

- When two objects touch, **electrons move** between them, but the **total charge** does not change.

# 3.1 Electric Charge — Resolved Exercises

## Electric Charge

### Exercise 1

An object gains electrons. What type of charge does it have?

**Solution:**

Gaining electrons gives an object a negative charge.

**Answer:** Negative charge (−).

---

### Exercise 2

Calculate the charge of 2 electrons.

**Solution:**

Formula:

**Q = n × e**

**Q = 2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹**

**Q = 3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ C**

**Answer:** The charge is **3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ C**.

# Electrons as Charge Carriers

### Exercise 1

A wire has a charge of **10 C** passing through it in **5 s**. Calculate the current.

**Solution:**

Formula:

**I = Q / t**

**I = 10 / 5**

**I = 2 A**

**Answer:** The current is **2 A**.
