# 2.4 Power

## Power

- **Power** is the **rate of using or transferring energy**.
    
- It tells us **how fast energy is used**.
    

**Equation:**

$$  
P = \frac{E}{t}
$$

#### The formula can also be: **P = V (voltage) * I (current)**

- **P** = Power (**W**)
    
- **E** = Energy (**J**)
    
- **t** = Time (**s**)
    

**Example:**

- A lamp uses **100 J** of energy in **5 s**.
    

$$  
P = \frac{100}{5} = 20\ \text{W}  
$$

The lamp has a **power of 20 W**.

---

## Power = Energy / Time

- A device that uses **more energy in less time** has **higher power**.
    
- A device that uses **the same energy over a longer time** has **lower power**.
    

**Example:**

- Device A: **100 J** in **5 s**
    

$$  
P = \frac{100}{5} = 20\ \text{W}  
$$

- Device B: **100 J** in **10 s**
    

$$  
P = \frac{100}{10} = 10\ \text{W}  
$$

**Device A** is more powerful because it uses energy **faster**.

---

## Watt as Unit of Computing Consumption

- The **watt (W)** is the **unit of power**.
    
- It shows **how much electrical power** a computer or device uses.
    
- **1 watt = 1 joule per second**.
    

**Equation:**

$$  
1\ \text{W} = 1\ \text{J/s}  
$$

**Example:**

- A **60 W** monitor uses **60 J** of energy every **second**.
    

---

## Heat Generation in Electronics

- Electronic devices **do not convert all energy into useful work**.
    
- Some electrical energy becomes **heat**.
    
- **CPUs** and **GPUs** need **cooling systems** to remove this heat.
    

**Example:**

- A CPU uses **80 W** of power.
    
- Most of this power is eventually released as **heat**, so a **fan** or **heat sink** keeps the CPU cool.
