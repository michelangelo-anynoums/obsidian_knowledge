# 1.3 Scientific Notation

## Scientific Notation

**Scientific notation** is a short way to write **very large** or **very small numbers**. It uses **powers of 10** to make numbers easier to read and calculate.

- **Large number:** **4.5 × 10⁶** = 4,500,000
    
- **Small number:** **3.2 × 10⁻⁶** = 0.0000032
    

## Why it is useful

**Scientific notation** is used in **physics** and **computer science** because it makes **calculations easier** and helps write **very big or very small values** without many zeros.

Examples:

- **Nanometer:** **1 nm = 1 × 10⁻⁹ m**
    
- **Gigahertz:** **1 GHz = 1 × 10⁹ Hz**

# Scientific Notation Examples

## Multiplication

When **multiplying**, multiply the **numbers** and **add the exponents**.

Example:

**(2 × 10³) × (4 × 10²)**

= **8 × 10⁵**

## Division

When **dividing**, divide the **numbers** and **subtract the exponents**.

Example:

**(8 × 10⁵) ÷ (2 × 10²)**

= **4 × 10³**

## Addition

When **adding**, the **powers of 10 must be the same**.

Example:

**3 × 10⁴ + 2 × 10⁴**

= **5 × 10⁴**

If the powers are different, change one number first.

Example:

**3 × 10⁴ + 4 × 10³**

= **3 × 10⁴ + 0.4 × 10⁴**

= **3.4 × 10⁴**

## Subtraction

When **subtracting**, the **powers of 10 must be the same**.

Example:

**7 × 10⁵ − 2 × 10⁵**

= **5 × 10⁵**
