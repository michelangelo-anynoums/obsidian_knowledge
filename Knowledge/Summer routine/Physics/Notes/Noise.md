## **What is Noise?**

**Noise** is **unwanted random signals** that interfere with the **desired signal**.

Noise makes it **harder to receive or understand information**.

**Examples:**

- Static on a radio.
- Crackling during a phone call.
- Electrical interference from nearby devices.

# **Signal Degradation**

**Signal degradation** means that a signal becomes **weaker or less clear** as it travels.

A signal can degrade because of:

- **Noise**
- **Long distances**
- **Obstacles** (walls, buildings)
- **Weather** (for some wireless signals)

**Example:**  
A Wi-Fi signal becomes weaker when you move far from the router.

---

# **Noise in Wireless Systems**

Wireless communication uses **radio waves**.

Noise can:

- Reduce **signal quality**.
- Cause **errors** in transmitted data.
- Slow down communication.
- Increase the number of **lost packets**.

---

# **Importance in Hacking Wireless Systems**

In **wireless security testing** (ethical hacking), noise is important because it can:

- Make wireless signals **harder to capture**.
- Increase **transmission errors**.
- Reduce the quality of collected data.
- Affect the **reliability of communication**.

Security professionals try to **measure** and **reduce** noise to better understand how a wireless network performs.

> **Note:** Ethical hacking is performed **only with permission** to improve the security of a system.

---

# **Important Ideas**

- **Noise** = **unwanted interference**.
- **Signal degradation** = the signal becomes **weaker or less clear**.
- Noise can reduce **signal quality** and cause **data errors**.
- Wireless systems work better when **noise is low**.
- In **ethical hacking**, understanding noise helps evaluate the **performance and security** of wireless networks.

# **Resolved Examples**

## **Example 1**

A radio signal has a lot of static.

**What caused the problem?**

**Solution**

The static is **noise** interfering with the radio signal.

**Answer:** **Noise reduces the signal quality.**

---

## **Example 2**

A person moves farther away from a Wi-Fi router.

**What happens to the signal?**

**Solution**

As the distance increases, the signal becomes weaker.

This is **signal degradation**.

**Answer:** **The Wi-Fi signal becomes weaker and less reliable.**

---

## **Example 3**

A security engineer tests a wireless network and notices many data errors because of interference.

**What is the likely cause?**

**Solution**

The interference is **noise**, which reduces the signal quality and increases transmission errors.

**Answer:** **Noise causes signal degradation and data errors.**
