# **2.1 Force**

## **Interaction between objects**

- A **force** is a **push** or a **pull**.
    
- Forces happen when **objects interact**.
    
- A force can change an object's **motion**, **speed**, or **direction**.
    

## **Newton's laws (basic intuition only)**

>**Newton's First Law (Inertia)**: An object stays at rest or keeps moving at a constant speed unless an outside force acts on it. **Example**: When a car stops suddenly, your body continues moving forward. 

>**Newton's Second Law (Force and Acceleration)**: The force applied to an object equals its mass multiplied by its acceleration ($F = ma$). **Example**: It requires more force to push a heavy shopping cart than a light one to achieve the same speed. 

>**Newton's Third Law (Action and Reaction)**: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. **Example**: When you jump, you push down on the ground, and the ground pushes you up.

## Interaction Between Objects

### Exercise 1

A person pushes a box with a force. What type of interaction is happening?

**Solution:**

A force is a push or a pull between objects.

**Answer:** The person and the box are interacting through a **force**.

---

### Exercise 2

A football is kicked and changes direction. Has a force acted on it?

**Solution:**

A force can change an object's motion, speed, or direction.

**Answer:** Yes, the kick applies a **force** that changes the football's direction.

---

### Exercise 3

A book is resting on a table and does not move. Are there forces acting on it?

**Solution:**

Yes. The table pushes upward, and gravity pulls downward.

**Answer:** Yes, forces are acting, but they are balanced, so the book does not move.

# Newton's First Law (Inertia)

### Exercise 1

A car stops suddenly, but passengers move forward. Why?

**Solution:**

Newton's First Law says an object keeps moving unless an outside force acts on it.

**Answer:** The passengers continue moving because of **inertia**.

---

### Exercise 2

A ball is sitting still on the ground. What will happen if no force acts on it?

**Solution:**

An object at rest stays at rest if no external force acts on it.

**Answer:** The ball will remain **still**.

### Exercise 3

A skateboard is moving at a constant speed. What does Newton's First Law say?

**Solution:**

An object keeps moving at a constant speed unless a force changes its motion.

**Answer:** The skateboard will continue moving at the same speed if no force acts on it.

# Newton's Second Law (Force and Acceleration)

### Exercise 1

A box has a mass of **5 kg** and accelerates at **2 m/s²**. Calculate the force.

**Solution:**

Formula:

**F = m × a**

Values:

- m = 5 kg
    
- a = 2 m/s²
    

**F = 5 × 2**

**F = 10 N**

**Answer:** The force is **10 N**.

---

### Exercise 2

A car has a mass of **1000 kg** and accelerates at **3 m/s²**. Calculate the force.

**Solution:**

Formula:

**F = m × a**

**F = 1000 × 3**

**F = 3000 N**

**Answer:** The force is **3000 N**.

---

### Exercise 3

Two shopping carts have the same acceleration. Cart A is heavier than Cart B. Which needs more force?

**Solution:**

Newton's Second Law:

**F = m × a**

A larger mass requires a larger force for the same acceleration.

**Answer:** Cart A needs more force.

---

# Newton's Third Law (Action and Reaction)

### Exercise 1

A person jumps upward. What forces are involved?

**Solution:**

The person pushes down on the ground. The ground pushes back with an equal force.

**Answer:** The action is pushing down, and the reaction is the ground pushing up.

---

### Exercise 2

A rocket moves upward after burning fuel. Explain why.

**Solution:**

The rocket pushes gases downward. The gases push the rocket upward.

**Answer:** The upward movement is caused by an **action-reaction pair**.

---

### Exercise 3

A swimmer pushes water backward while swimming. What happens?

**Solution:**

The swimmer applies a force to the water, and the water applies an opposite force.

**Answer:** The swimmer moves forward because the water pushes them forward.
