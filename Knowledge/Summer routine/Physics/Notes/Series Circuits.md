# 4.1 Series Circuits

## **What is a series circuit?**

A **series circuit** is a circuit where all components are connected **one after another** in a single path.

- There is **only one path** for the current.
    
- If one component breaks, the **whole circuit stops**.
    

**Example:** A flashlight with one battery and one bulb.

---

## **Same current through all components**

In a series circuit, the **current** is the same through every component.

**Formula:**

```text
I = I₁ = I₂ = I₃
```

Where:

- **I** = current (A, amperes)
    

**Example:**

If the current is **2 A**, then:

```text
I₁ = I₂ = I₃ = 2 A
```

---

## **Voltage divides across components**

The **total voltage** is shared between all components.

**Formula:**

```text
Vtotal = V₁ + V₂ + V₃
```

Where:

- **Vtotal** = battery (source) voltage
    
- **V₁, V₂, V₃** = voltage across each component
    

**Example:**

Battery voltage = **12 V**

```text
V₁ = 5 V
V₂ = 7 V

Vtotal = 5 V + 7 V = 12 V
```

---

## **Equivalent resistance increases**

The **equivalent resistance** is the sum of all resistors.

**Formula:**

```text
Req = R₁ + R₂ + R₃
```

Where:

- **Req** = equivalent (total) resistance
    
- **R₁, R₂, R₃** = individual resistances
    

**Example:**

```text
R₁ = 4 Ω
R₂ = 6 Ω

Req = 4 Ω + 6 Ω = 10 Ω
```

Adding more resistors in series makes the **total resistance larger**.

---

# **Important Facts**

- **Current is the same** through every component.
    
- **Voltage is divided** across the components.
    
- **Resistances add together**.
    
- If one component breaks, the **entire circuit stops**.

# **Resolved Examples**

## **Example 1: Current**

**Question:**  
A series circuit has a current of **3 A**. What is the current through each resistor?

**Solution:**

In a series circuit, the current is the same everywhere.

```text
I = I₁ = I₂ = I₃

I = 3 A
```

**Answer:**

```text
I₁ = I₂ = I₃ = 3 A
```

---

## **Example 2: Equivalent Resistance**

**Question:**  
Three resistors are connected in series.

```text
R₁ = 2 Ω
R₂ = 5 Ω
R₃ = 3 Ω
```

Find the equivalent resistance.

**Solution:**

Use the formula:

```text
Req = R₁ + R₂ + R₃
```

Substitute the values:

```text
Req = 2 Ω + 5 Ω + 3 Ω
Req = 10 Ω
```

**Answer:**

```text
Req = 10 Ω
```

---

## **Example 3: Voltage Division**

**Question:**  
A battery provides **15 V**. One resistor has a voltage drop of **9 V**. What is the voltage across the second resistor?

**Solution:**

Use the voltage formula:

```text
Vtotal = V₁ + V₂
```

Substitute the values:

```text
15 V = 9 V + V₂
```

Solve for **V₂**:

```text
V₂ = 15 V − 9 V
V₂ = 6 V
```

**Answer:**

```text
V₂ = 6 V
```
