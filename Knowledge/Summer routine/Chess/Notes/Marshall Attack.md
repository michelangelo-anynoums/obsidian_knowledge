
The **Marshall Attack** (7...0-0 8.c3 d5) is a sharp, aggressive gambit in the Ruy Lopez where Black sacrifices a pawn to seize the initiative and launch a kingside attack. Instead of accepting the pawn immediately, White often declines it to avoid heavy theoretical lines, but the main line continues **9.exd5 Nxd5 10.Nxe5 Nxe5 11.Rxe5 c6**, preparing ...Qd6 to target White's king.

Famous since **Frank Marshall**'s prepared novelty against **José Raúl Capablanca** in 1918, the line remains a critical weapon for players seeking dynamic counterplay. Despite Capablanca eventually winning that historic game, the attack proved sound and has been employed by elite grandmasters like **Boris Spassky**, **Peter Leko** (who defeated **Vladimir Kramnik** with it in 2004), and **Levon Aronian**.
