# Wi-Fi Standards

**Wi-Fi standards** are defined by the **IEEE 802.11** protocol family, which specifies the medium access control and physical layer protocols for wireless local area networks.  

Since 2018, the **Wi-Fi Alliance** has used a simplified generational numbering scheme (Wi-Fi 1 through Wi-Fi 8) to market these underlying IEEE standards to consumers.

**IEEE 802.11** is a set of media access control (MAC) and physical layer (PHY) specifications

# Channel Width and Interference

## **Channel Width**

- **Channel width** is the amount of frequency space used by a Wi-Fi signal.
    
- A wider channel allows **more data** to be sent at the same time.
    
- Common channel widths are **20 MHz, 40 MHz, 80 MHz, and 160 MHz**.
    
- Wider channels provide **higher speed**, but they can cause more interference.
    

## **Interference**

- **Interference** happens when other signals disturb a Wi-Fi connection.
    
- It can come from **other Wi-Fi networks**, **Bluetooth devices**, microwaves, or other electronic devices.
    
- Interference can reduce **speed**, **range**, and **connection stability**.
    
- Using a less crowded **Wi-Fi channel** can help reduce interference.

---

## **802.11a**

- Released in **1999**.
    
- Uses the **5 GHz** frequency band.
    
- Speed: up to **54 Mbps**.
    
- Has less interference but shorter range.
    
- Mostly replaced by newer standards.
    

## **802.11b**

- Released in **1999**.
    
- Uses the **2.4 GHz** frequency band.
    
- Speed: up to **11 Mbps**.
    
- Has a longer range but is slower.
    
- Was one of the first popular Wi-Fi standards.
    

## **802.11g**

- Released in **2003**.
    
- Uses the **2.4 GHz** frequency band.
    
- Speed: up to **54 Mbps**.
    
- Combines the speed of 802.11a with the range of 802.11b.
    
- Compatible with **802.11b** devices.
    

## **802.11n (Wi-Fi 4)**

- Released in **2009**.
    
- Uses **2.4 GHz and 5 GHz** bands.
    
- Speed: up to **600 Mbps**.
    
- Introduced **MIMO** technology (multiple antennas).
    
- Provides better speed and range.
    

## **802.11ac (Wi-Fi 5)**

- Released in **2013**.
    
- Uses the **5 GHz** band.
    
- Speed: up to several **Gbps**.
    
- Uses wider channels and **MU-MIMO** technology.
    
- Designed for high-speed networks.
    

## **802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6)**

- Released in **2019**.
    
- Uses **2.4 GHz and 5 GHz** bands.
    
- Speed: up to about **9.6 Gbps**.
    
- Improves performance in crowded areas.
    
- Uses **OFDMA** and better efficiency technologies.
