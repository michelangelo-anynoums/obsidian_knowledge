# Wi-Fi Bands + Channels

![[Pasted image 20260722082132.png|527]]
## **2.4 GHz**

- **2.4 GHz** is the oldest and most common Wi-Fi band.
    
- It has **longer range** but **slower speed**.
    
- It works better through **walls**.
    
- More devices use it, so it has **more interference**.
    

## **5 GHz**

- **5 GHz** gives **faster speeds** than 2.4 GHz.
    
- It has **less interference**.
    
- Its **range is shorter**.
    
- It is good for **streaming** and **online gaming**.
    

## **6 GHz**

- **6 GHz** is the newest Wi-Fi band.
    
- It offers the **highest speeds** and **lowest interference**.
    
- It has **many more channels**.
    
- It requires **Wi-Fi 6E** or **Wi-Fi 7** devices.
    

## **Channels 1, 6, 11**

- A **channel** is a path used to send Wi-Fi signals.
    
- In **2.4 GHz**, **Channels 1, 6, and 11** do **not overlap**.
    
- Using these channels gives a **more stable connection**.
    

## **Channel Overlap**

- **Channel overlap** happens when nearby Wi-Fi networks use **overlapping channels**.
    
- This causes **interference** and **slower performance**.
    
- Using **Channels 1, 6, or 11** helps reduce overlap in the **2.4 GHz** band.

## **What is a Channel?**

![[Pasted image 20260722082150.png|520]]

A **channel** is a **small range of radio frequencies** used to carry Wi-Fi signals.

Wi-Fi sends information using **electromagnetic waves** (radio waves).

Every radio wave has a **frequency**, which is how many times the wave oscillates every second.

- **Frequency** is measured in **Hertz (Hz)**.
    
- **1 GHz = 1 billion oscillations per second.**
    

A **Wi-Fi band** (such as **2.4 GHz**) contains **many frequencies**.

Instead of using the whole band at once, Wi-Fi divides it into **smaller frequency ranges**. Each of these ranges is called a **channel**.

### **Simple Example**

Imagine the **2.4 GHz band** is a **long piano keyboard**.

- The **whole keyboard** = the **Wi-Fi band**.
    
- A **few neighboring piano keys** = **one channel**.
    

A Wi-Fi router does **not** use the entire keyboard. It plays only a **small group of keys** (one channel).

### **Example with Numbers**

The **2.4 GHz** band goes from about:

- **2.400 GHz** to **2.4835 GHz**
    

Inside this band are many channels.

For example:

- **Channel 1** is centered at about **2.412 GHz**.
    
- **Channel 6** is centered at about **2.437 GHz**.
    
- **Channel 11** is centered at about **2.462 GHz**.
    

Each channel uses a **small range of frequencies**, not just one exact frequency.

### **Why Do Channels Overlap?**

A Wi-Fi signal is **not infinitely thin**. It spreads over a range of frequencies.

Think of drawing a line with a **thick marker** instead of a pencil.

- The **center** of the marker is the **channel number**.
    
- The **ink around it** is the range of frequencies used.
    

If two channels are **too close together**, their frequency ranges **overlap**.

When this happens, the radio waves mix together and create **interference**.

That is why **Channels 1, 6, and 11** are commonly used—they are far enough apart that their frequency ranges **do not overlap**.
