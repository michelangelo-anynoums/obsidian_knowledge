## **RSSI (Received Signal Strength Indicator)**

- **RSSI** shows how strong a received wireless signal is.
- It measures the power level of a signal received by a device.
- A **higher RSSI value** means a stronger signal.

## **dBm (decibel-milliwatts)**

- **dBm** is a unit used to measure signal power.
- It shows the strength of a signal compared to **1 milliwatt (mW)**.
- Values are usually negative: **closer to 0 dBm = stronger signal**.

## **SNR (Signal-to-Noise Ratio)**

- **SNR** compares the useful signal with the background noise.
- A **high SNR** means a clear and reliable connection.
- A **low SNR** means more noise and poorer communication.

## **Interference**

- **Interference** is unwanted energy that disrupts a signal.
- It can come from other devices, networks, or electronic equipment.
- More interference can cause **slower speeds** and **connection problems**.
