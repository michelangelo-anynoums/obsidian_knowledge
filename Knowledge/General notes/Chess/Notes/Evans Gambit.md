# Evans Gambit

## A Short History

The **Evans Gambit** is an exciting chess opening. It is part of the **Italian Game**.

The opening is named after **Captain William Davies Evans**, a sea captain from Wales. He introduced the gambit in the early 1800s.

In the 19th century, many famous players used the Evans Gambit to create fast attacks. Today, it is played less often at the highest level, but many players still enjoy it because it is fun, aggressive, and teaches attacking chess.

---

## The Evans Gambit

### Opening Moves

1. e4 e5
    
2. Nf3 Nc6
    
3. Bc4 Bc5
    
4. b4
    

White offers the **b-pawn** as a sacrifice. This is called a **gambit**.

If Black accepts the pawn:

4...Bxb4  
5. c3

White attacks the bishop and gains time to build a strong centre.

### Main Line: 4...Bxb4 5.c3 Ba5

Black's usual response is to accept the gambit with **4...Bxb4. 4...Nxb4** is less flexible as **5.c3 Nc6** effectively transposes into the inferior retreat **4...Bxb4 5.c3** **Bc5.** After **5.c3** the usual retreat is **5...Ba5,** which pins the pawn on **c3** if White plays **6.d4,** but it has the drawback of removing the option of moving to the square **a5** from the black knight. Later, Black will often retreat the bishop to **b6** in order to facilitate **...Na5.** White then usually follows up with **6.d4,** entailing a second pawn offer; **6.0-0** and **6.Qb3** are alternatives.

### Main Ideas

- Sacrifice a pawn for quick development.
    
- Gain time by attacking Black's bishop.
    
- Control the centre with pawns.
    
- Develop pieces quickly.
    
- Attack before Black finishes developing.
    

### White's Plan

- Play **c3** and **d4** to control the centre.
    
- Develop all the pieces quickly.
    
- Castle early.
    
- Open lines for the bishops and queen.
    
- Create a strong attack against Black's king.
    

### Black's Plan

- Decide whether to accept or decline the gambit.
    
- Develop pieces carefully.
    
- Return the extra pawn if needed to complete development.
    
- Castle early and keep the king safe.
    
- Defend against White's attack before looking for counterplay.
    

---


The Evans Gambit is one of the most famous attacking openings in chess. It helps players learn about development, initiative, and active piece play.
