The **Berlin Defence** (3...Nf6) is Black's most popular alternative in the Ruy Lopez, renowned for its **solidity** and high draw rate.  Historically common in the late 19th and early 20th centuries among players like **Emanuel Lasker**, its popularity waned for decades due to perceptions of passivity.

The opening experienced a dramatic **renaissance** in 2000 when **Vladimir Kramnik** successfully employed it as a "drawing weapon" against **Garry Kasparov** in the World Championship.  This performance earned the main line the nickname "**Berlin Wall**" and re-established it as a cornerstone of elite chess, frequently used by top grandmasters like **Magnus Carlsen** to neutralize White's attacking chances.
