# 8.4 Doping

## **Doping**

**Doping** is the process of adding a small amount of **impurity atoms** to a pure semiconductor (usually **silicon**) to change its electrical properties.

Pure silicon does not conduct electricity very well because it has few **free electrons**. Doping increases the number of charge carriers, making silicon more useful in electronic devices.

Example:

- A small amount of another element is added to silicon.
    
- The silicon can then conduct electricity better.
    

---

## **Adding impurities to silicon**

Silicon has **4 valence electrons**. It forms strong bonds with other silicon atoms.

When we add impurity atoms:

- Some impurities create extra **free electrons**.
    
- Other impurities create **holes** (missing electrons).
    

These impurities are added in very small amounts, but they greatly change the behaviour of silicon.

---

## **P-type materials**

A **P-type semiconductor** is made by adding atoms with **3 valence electrons** to silicon.

Common impurity: **boron (B)**.

- Boron has one less electron than silicon.
    
- This creates a **hole** (a place where an electron is missing).
    
- Holes act like positive charges.
    

Main charge carrier:

$$  
\textbf{holes (+)}  
$$

Example:

- Silicon + boron → P-type silicon
    

---

## **N-type materials**

An **N-type semiconductor** is made by adding atoms with **5 valence electrons** to silicon.

Common impurity: **phosphorus (P)**.

- Phosphorus has one extra electron.
    
- The extra electron becomes a **free electron**.
    
- Electrons move and carry electric current.
    

Main charge carrier:

$$
\textbf{electrons (-)}  
$$

Example:

- Silicon + phosphorus → N-type silicon
    

---

# Simple comparison

|Material|Added impurity|Main carrier|Charge|
|---|---|---|---|
|**P-type**|Boron|Holes|Positive (+)|
|**N-type**|Phosphorus|Electrons|Negative (-)|

---

# Important idea

**Doping controls the electrical properties of silicon.**

It is used to make electronic components such as:

- **diodes**
    
- **transistors**
    
- **computer chips**
    

---

# Resolved examples

## Example 1: Finding the material type

**Question:**  
Silicon is mixed with phosphorus. Is it P-type or N-type?

**Solution:**

1. Phosphorus has **5 valence electrons**.
    
2. It gives an extra electron to silicon.
    
3. Extra electrons are the main carriers.
    

**Answer:**

$$
\boxed{\text{N-type semiconductor}}  
$$

---

## Example 2: Finding the charge carrier

**Question:**  
A semiconductor contains boron impurities. What carries the current?

**Solution:**

1. Boron has **3 valence electrons**.
    
2. It creates missing electrons called **holes**.
    
3. Holes behave like positive charges.
    

**Answer:**

$$
\boxed{\text{Holes carry the current}}  
$$

---

## Example 3: Simple conductivity idea

**Question:**  
Why does doped silicon conduct electricity better than pure silicon?

**Solution:**

Pure silicon:

$$
\text{few free carriers} \rightarrow \text{low conductivity}  
$$

Doped silicon:

$$
\text{more electrons or holes} \rightarrow \text{higher conductivity}  
$$

**Answer:**

Doping increases the number of charge carriers, so silicon becomes a better conductor.
