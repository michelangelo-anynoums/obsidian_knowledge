# 1. Fundamentals of Soldering

## What is soldering?

- **Soldering** is the process of **joining two metal surfaces** using a **filler metal** called **solder**.
    
- It creates both an **electrical connection** and a **mechanical connection**.
    
- It is **not just gluing wires together**.
    

## Key concepts

### **Wetting**

- **Wetting** means the **molten solder flows smoothly** over the metal.
    
- Good wetting creates a **strong and reliable joint**.
    

### **Heat transfer**

- The **joint** must be heated first.
    
- The solder should **melt because the joint is hot**, not because the soldering iron touches the solder.
    

### **Clean surfaces**

- Metal surfaces must be **clean**.
    
- **Oxidation** or dirt prevents the solder from bonding well.
    

### **Capillary action**

- **Capillary action** makes **molten solder flow into small gaps** between metal parts.
    
- This helps create a **strong connection**.

# Wetting, Surface Tension, and Capillarity

### 1. Wetting

**Wetting** is the ability of a liquid to spread over a solid surface.

- A liquid **wets** a surface when its attraction to the solid is strong.
- Example: **water spreads on clean glass**.
- If the attraction is weak, the liquid forms **drops** instead.

### 2. Surface Tension

**Surface tension** is the tendency of a liquid surface to behave like a thin, stretched skin.

- It is caused by **attractive forces between liquid molecules**.
- It makes the liquid surface try to have the **smallest possible area**.
- Example: **small water drops are nearly spherical**.
- Surface tension also allows some small insects to **walk on water**.

### 3. Capillarity

**Capillarity** is the movement of a liquid through a **very narrow tube or space**.

- A liquid can **rise or fall** in a thin tube.
- It depends on **adhesion**, **cohesion**, and **surface tension**.
- Example: **water rises in a thin glass tube**.
- Capillarity helps plants **move water from their roots to their leaves**.
